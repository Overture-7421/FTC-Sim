# The BIOBUZZ programs

What the stand-in programs for the BIOBUZZ robot prove (the diagnostic stub and the two alliance TeleOps that tip a HIVE) and how to run them in the window; read it before trusting a BIOBUZZ robot you have not yet written code for.

There is no BIOBUZZ robot program from the team yet. These are written as a team writes one, knowing nothing of the simulator, in `acceptance-tests\src\scenarios\java\dev\ftcsim\scenarios\opmodes`:

| Program | What it is |
|---|---|
| `BiobuzzRedTeleOp` ("BIOBUZZ Red"), `BiobuzzBlueTeleOp` ("BIOBUZZ Blue") | The driver programs, one per alliance, over the shared `BiobuzzAllianceTeleOp`. **Red aims at the red HIVE and blue at the blue one**; the only other difference is which way is forward. **The left stick drives the field as the driver sees it from their wall**: up is away from the driver (red looks towards +Y, blue towards -Y) and right is the driver's right, whichever way the robot faces (from the pose estimate's heading, so start facing the field as the rules say); the right stick turns the robot. **Hold the left bumper to take balls in, hold the right bumper to shoot**; the intake and the feed stop once let go, while the flywheels always idle at the speed of the shot. The intake roller also runs while the shooter feeds, because the lowest of the four queued balls is moved along only by it (the indexer starts 0.15 m up the path). The turret and the hood always follow the HIVE's CELL that faces up. The drive, intake and indexer motors are on brake, so they stop at once when the power goes |
| `BiobuzzStubTeleOp` ("BIOBUZZ Stub") | The diagnostic, everything by hand: mecanum drive on the sticks, a goBILDA Pinpoint called `odopods`, a Limelight called `limelight`, a `CRServo` `turret`, an `OctoQuad` `octoquad` for the turret's two absolute encoders, and the intake (`intake`), indexer (`indexer`), flywheels (`shooterLeft`, `shooterRight`) and four positional servos (`hood`, `brake`, `colibriLeft`, `colibriRight`). Bumpers turn the turret, left trigger intake, right trigger flywheels, B the indexer, X the stopper, Y the colibri arms, the D-pad the hood. It shows what it learns on telemetry |
| `BiobuzzFlowerAuto` ("BIOBUZZ Flower Demo") | Select RED wall, centre and four POLLEN preloads. Start the autonomous and watch Stage and Result on telemetry. It turns the turret using the OctoQuad and fires all four preloads. It drives to the red-wall FLOWER using Pinpoint and runs the intake while the joined colibri plate nudges the stack from below. One four-degree lift releases the four POLLEN. The intake and indexer run for at least two seconds while the stack falls, and the upper sensor confirms pickup. A stalled stage times out and stops the motors. |
| `BiobuzzHardware` | The hardware lookup and set-up both programs share: the Pinpoint with the CAD's pod offsets, the Limelight, the OctoQuad, the mecanum drive and the turret's angle from its two encoders |
| `BiobuzzTeamTablesRedTeleOp` ("BIOBUZZ Team Tables Red"), `BiobuzzTeamTablesBlueTeleOp` | The same driver programs with the shot of the team's own code in place of the solver's (`BiobuzzTeamTables`: the flywheel and hood tables of `LaunchConstants` against the distance in inches from the turret's pivot to the CELL, read as SolversLib's `InterpLUT` does, and the team's `Hive` target; nothing else of the team's code runs). `BiobuzzShotPlan` is the seam: the shooter sequence takes its aim, hood and speed from it. `BiobuzzTeamTablesSessionTest` plays it into the HIVE from six places on the open side of each alliance's HIVE (41 to 72 in from the CELL; the near rows of the table, since the field has no legal place further than about 87 in and the camera does not see the tags from beyond y = 1.1) |
| `BiobuzzHive` | The HIVEs as plain numbers: both alliances, both tilts, every AprilTag, and the CELL's geometry |
| `BiobuzzPoseEstimator` | The Pinpoint's odometry corrected by the Limelight's 3D targets |
| `BiobuzzShotSolver` | Where to aim, how steeply and how fast, and whether the ball goes in |
| `BiobuzzHiveTracker` | Which way a HIVE is tipped, from its tags' height |
| `BiobuzzHoldShooter` | The hold-to-shoot sequence: no hardware, told what the robot measures and answering what every actuator does |

The plain classes (`BiobuzzHive`, `BiobuzzPoseEstimator`, `BiobuzzShotSolver`, `BiobuzzHiveTracker`, `BiobuzzHoldShooter`) are Java 8 without the SDK, as a robot project compiles with `-source 8` (no records, no `List.of`); `BiobuzzPoseEstimatorTest` compiles them with `--release 8`.

## How a HIVE is tipped

A HIVE has two CELLs and rests on one of its stops, 30 degrees from level, so one CELL faces up: at the start the red HIVE's audience CELL and the blue HIVE's scoring CELL ([biobuzz](../fields/biobuzz.md)). **Only that CELL can be loaded to tip the HIVE**, and a CELL is a small house **open only at the far end of its arm**, so the ball has to come down into it: a steep lob, 55 degrees from far to 85 degrees from near. The robot therefore shoots from the side of the HIVE its CELL opens towards (the audience side for red, the scoring side for blue at the start). Three POLLEN on top of the three NECTAR the CELL starts with tip it (the field's calibration: a TIP at 3 NECTAR and 3 POLLEN, none at 2), and the robot carries four, its preloads, so one is left after a TIP.

- **Targeting.** `BiobuzzShotSolver` takes a point in the CELL's mouth. For every launch angle the hood can make it works out the speed that goes through that point, flies the ball (a POLLEN, no drag) against the CELL's floor, back, sides and roof, and keeps the angles at which it still goes in with the turret 1.5 degrees off, the flywheels 3 percent fast or slow and the hood 0.7 degrees off. It takes the hood to the nearest of those angles that works with a degree to spare on either side (not to the middle of them), and leaves it where it is while where it is still works, because the hood's servo (a Super Speed, 300 degrees, about 1130 degrees a second, 0.0044 s a degree of hood) is the slowest thing in the robot: it moves as little as it has to, the shot is worked out again every 0.2 s or centimetre while the robot drives, and the hood is moving towards its next angle all the time. When there is no shot it says why (`WRONG_SIDE`, `TOO_CLOSE`, `OUT_OF_RANGE`, `NO_ARC`) and the telemetry line `Aim` says where to go.
- **The shot.** `BiobuzzHoldShooter`: everything is done as fast as the hardware lets it. **The flywheels are never off**: from Start they run at the speed of the shot, kept in step with where the robot stands (a stiff P loop on a feed-forward, full power until they are within a hair of it), so nothing has to spin up when the button goes down. With no shot from here (the wrong side of the HIVE, too near, or the pose only the start) they idle at the speed of a 65 degree lob to the CELL's mouth from where the robot is (`BiobuzzShotSolver.nominalTicks`), close to what a shot from nearby needs, and 1500 ticks a second if even that is out of reach. While the right bumper is held the stopper opens at once, from 0.5 to 1.0 (150 to 300 degrees of its Super Speed servo, the team's calibrated shut and open positions; it lets a ball by from 225, so it travels about 0.04 s before releasing); when the turret is within 2 degrees, the flywheels within 3 percent for 0.05 s, the hood's servo has had time to arrive and the robot is not driving, and the stopper has had its 0.15 s, the indexer feeds: full power for the first 0.035 s, which takes the first ball up to the flywheels, then 0.1, because at full power the four balls leave 20 ms apart, fly in a line, touch in the air and the last falls short (the flywheels themselves lose under 1 percent a ball), while at 0.1 the balls have time to separate and all four go through the CELL's mouth (`BiobuzzAllianceSessionTest`); it pauses while the flywheels sag or the robot moves. The first ball leaves under 0.9 s after the press with the hood and turret already on target (`BiobuzzAllianceSessionTest`). Letting go shuts the stopper and stops the indexer at once; the flywheels keep their speed. It refuses, with the reason on `Shooter`, while the pose is unconfirmed or no throw gets in. The turret is given full power (about 510 degrees a second of the Axon Mini+ behind 0.99:1, the most the servo does) with a P loop that brakes as it arrives.
- **The pose starts approximate.** Each alliance's OpMode assumes the centre start on its wall (the red wall's is x 0, y -1.557 facing +Y; blue is the same turned half a turn); the robot may stand anywhere on it. Camera frames are about 0.1 s old (latency plus the wait for the next frame), so the OpModes leave them out of the pose while the odometry says the robot moves faster than 0.2 m/s (the tilt of the HIVE, which needs no pose, is read from every frame, so a HIVE tipped on the far side is noticed in passing), and the turret's angle is read from the two encoders without wrapping it to ±180 (`BiobuzzHardware.turretDegrees` gives -180 to about +277), so that a turret that has gone past half a turn to the left is read as that, not as a negative angle the controller would wind towards; its target is always put inside the window the two encoders can decode (-170 to +250 degrees), going the long way round when the HIVE is behind the stop: outside it the decode is wrong and the aim runs away. The estimator gives way entirely to the first Limelight fix of two tags or more (and is `confirmed` from then), keeps the blends of Hornet's `Camera` after that (0.6 position and heading when the tags are within 3 m, 0.4 and 0.9 beyond), ignores a fix more than a metre or 30 degrees from the estimate, and replaces the estimate after eight different frames in a row that agree with one another but not with it. Until the first fix the turret sweeps 100 degrees either side of where the HIVE should be (under the 40 degrees a second above which frames are left out) and shooting is refused. The simulator's Pinpoint itself is exact; the simulated turret has no mechanical stop, which a real one has.
- **The camera sees the tags only from the side they face.** The tags are on the underside of the arm and face down and towards the end that is up: the red HIVE's, at the start, towards the audience. A red robot on the audience side sees them, which is also the side its CELL opens to; on the scoring side it cannot, so it stays on its approximate pose until it has driven round.
- **Tilt tracking.** The tags are about 1.26 m up on the end that is up and 0.90 m on the end that is down; a tag's height needs no pose, so `BiobuzzHiveTracker` takes six different frames in a row that agree on the other tilt before the target turns to the other CELL (and the estimator's tag map follows). After a TIP the tags face the other side, so **the robot sees the change only once it is on the new open side**; until then it keeps aiming at the old CELL.

Measured by the field ([fields/biobuzz.md](../fields/biobuzz.md) has its numbers): the red HIVE with its audience CELL up takes three POLLEN lobbed from the hood's exit (about 0.40 to 0.47 m high) from some places from x 0.4 m on, on the audience side (probed on a grid of 0.2 m, elevations 55 to 85 degrees, no robot). The solver keeps to shots that go in with the margins above, which on the red half are from about **0.7 m from the CELL's mouth** (x 0.9 to 1.0 on the line of the arm, x 1.2 from the wall corner) **to the end of the field**; nearer than that no lob in the hood's range is robust. `BiobuzzAllianceSessionTest` tips the red and the blue HIVE from five places each.

## What `BiobuzzAllianceSessionTest` and the others check

| Check | How |
|---|---|
| Holding the right bumper tips the alliance's own HIVE | Red and blue from x 1.0 to 1.7 and mirrored (near, side, far, corner, field end): the field's score says `HIVE TIP in TELEOP` 1, the HIVE rests on its other stop and the other alliance's has not moved |
| The approximate start is corrected | Facing the HIVE, the first frame confirms the pose within 5 cm and 3 degrees; on the wall facing away the pose stays approximate, the turret sweeps more than 100 degrees, and nothing is fed and the flywheels stay off |
| Turret, hood and flywheels follow with no button; the bumpers only work while held | Six seconds after Start the hood is where the shot wants it, the turret faces the CELL within 6 degrees and the flywheels are already at speed (also while the pose is only the start); the left bumper runs the intake and stages the balls at the shut stopper, and not shoots; the right bumper reaches FEEDING and, let go, shuts the stopper and stops the indexer, and the flywheels keep their speed; the four preloads wait low in the column at the start (`4 of 4 pieces`); the first ball leaves under 0.9 s after the press; the intake has stopped within 0.2 s of letting go |
| The left stick is the field as the driver sees it | Red: up on the stick goes towards +Y and right towards +X; blue the opposite; also after the robot has turned any amount |

| Unit tests | `BiobuzzShotSolverTest` (an independent flight, near steeper than far, the reasons, red against blue, the hood kept), `BiobuzzHiveTrackerTest` (switching, misreads, the same frame again, an arm in mid-swing, the other HIVE's tags), `BiobuzzHoldShooterTest` (the sequence, pauses, release, refusals), `BiobuzzPoseEstimatorTest` (placement from two tags at any turret angle, the blends, one tag, the approximate start, recovery, the tag map of a tipped HIVE, Java 8) |

## What the stub (`BiobuzzStubSessionTest`) checks

| Check | How |
|---|---|
| The robot drives as the sticks say | Forward moves ahead along the heading, left strafes to the robot's left, right stick turns clockwise (true chassis pose, red wall start) |
| The odopods work | The Pinpoint's X, Y and heading equal the true motion in the robot's start frame (within 15 mm and 3 degrees), and its pod X count follows the travel at the 4-bar pod resolution the stub sets |
| The Limelight sees the HIVE tags | Standing 1 m from tag 31 of the red HIVE, tags 30 to 33 are listed, tag 31 among them, `botpose` has no tags, and turning the robot 12 degrees to the left moves the target to the right in the image (`tx` rises by more than 6 degrees) |
| The turret turns and is read back | The bumpers turn the servo; the stub decodes the turret angle from the two absolute encoders on the OctoQuad (5.5 and 44/7 turns to one of the turret, the vernier of the two) and it matches the true angle within a degree from -165 to +250 degrees. The window's turret body is turned by the same angle |
| Every mechanism of the picture moves | The wheels turn forward when driving; the left trigger runs the intake, the right trigger the flywheel, B the indexer; X opens the stopper to 75 degrees of servo (it rests at 45), Y raises the colibri arms 90 degrees, the D-pad raises the hood and the encoder gear turns 5.5 times to the turret's one |
| The colour sensors | `upperSensor` and `lowerSensor` (looked up as `ColorRangeSensor` in `BiobuzzHardware`, shown as `Sensors : up=<cm> intake=<cm>` by the stub): four preloads, both near (under 5 cm); none left, both at the 15.2 cm maximum; the ball left near the intake, only `lowerSensor`; staged against the stopper, only `upperSensor` (`BiobuzzColourSensorsSessionTest`, [color-sensor](../devices/color-sensor.md)). Their mounts are estimates |
| The pose estimate | Standing 1 m from tag 31 the stub has the true pose within 5 cm and 3 degrees after a quarter of a second and keeps it driving away; facing the wall it has none; its map is the field's tags at the start of a match |

**What it cannot prove.** The Pinpoint is ideal ([pinpoint.md](../devices/pinpoint.md)): pod offsets and encoder directions are accepted and ignored, so a wrong offset in the real code does not show here. The offsets are the CAD's ([biobuzz-cad](biobuzz-cad.md)). The camera has no pose of its own on this field: every BIOBUZZ tag is on a HIVE, which tips, so `botpose` is empty ([limelight.md](../devices/limelight.md)); the estimator therefore carries its own map of the tags, `BiobuzzHive`. After a TIP the tags are up to 7 cm from the start map; the tracker switches the map. The hood is not the CAD's: its stop is widened to 44 degrees so that a CELL can be reached from near and far ([fixed-robots](fixed-robots.md)), and the real hood would need a mechanism that range. The flywheel numbers in `BiobuzzShotSolver` (0.49 of the rim speed) are the simulator's, to be replaced by the team's own shot table. The camera's frames are some tens of milliseconds old, so the programs leave out the frames taken while the turret turns faster than 40 degrees a second or the robot faster than 60. The shot is a lob from a robot that is standing still; there is no shooting on the move, no autonomous, no opposing robot.

## Running it in the window

1. Make a robot project for your SDK: clone FIRST's `FtcRobotController` at the release you use and open it as the project.
2. Copy `BiobuzzAllianceTeleOp.java`, `BiobuzzRedTeleOp.java`, `BiobuzzBlueTeleOp.java`, `BiobuzzHardware.java`, `BiobuzzHive.java`, `BiobuzzPoseEstimator.java`, `BiobuzzShotSolver.java`, `BiobuzzHiveTracker.java`, `BiobuzzHoldShooter.java` and, if you want the diagnostic, `BiobuzzStubTeleOp.java` into `TeamCode`, and change their package lines.
3. Nothing to map: BIOBUZZ's robot is built into the simulator ([fixed-robots.md](fixed-robots.md)) and the stub uses its names (`lf`, `lr`, `rf`, `rr`, `intake`, `indexer`, `shooterLeft`, `shooterRight`, `turret`, `hood`, `brake`, `colibriLeft`, `colibriRight`, `upperSensor`, `lowerSensor`; the Pinpoint, the OctoQuad and the Limelight are found by class). The turret, the two OctoQuad encoders, the four mechanism servos and the camera on the turret are all there.

A ready-made project, FIRST's `FtcRobotController` for the SDK of your choice with these files added, is the quickest way to try it: BIOBUZZ is an SDK 12 game, so use the `v12.0` tag.

4. **Open project**, pick BIOBUZZ for the game, start on your alliance's wall (the centre spot is what the program assumes; any is fine) and choose `BIOBUZZ Red` or `BIOBUZZ Blue`, Init, wait for the Pinpoint to say `READY`, Start. Drive to the side of your HIVE that its CELL opens to (the audience side for red, the scoring side for blue) and look at it: `Estimate` says `confirmed` once the camera has seen two tags, `Aim` says where it shoots and `Shooter` what it is waiting for. Stand still and hold the right bumper (`E` on the keyboard): the flywheels spin up and the balls go. Hold the left bumper (`Q`) to take balls in; they wait at the stopper.
